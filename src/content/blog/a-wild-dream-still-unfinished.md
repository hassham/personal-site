---
title: "A Wild Dream, Still Unfinished"
description: "A reflection on 18+ years across Karachi, Dubai, and Sydney — and why the story isn't finished yet."
date: 2026-08-07
---

If you had told the version of me growing up in Karachi that one day I'd be living in Sydney, working as a Senior Software Engineer with close to two decades of experience behind me, I don't think I would have believed you. It would have sounded less like a plan and more like a wild dream — the kind you have but don't quite let yourself take seriously, just in case it doesn't come true.

That dream took a long road. It started in Karachi, where I studied computer science and took my first steps into software development. It carried me to Dubai, into new teams, new problems, and new ways of thinking about the work. And eventually it brought me to Sydney, to a new country, a new culture, and yet another chapter of starting over — again.

Every one of those moves asked the same quiet question: are you willing to be a beginner again? Each time, the honest answer was "not really" — and I did it anyway. New city, new team, new expectations, sometimes a new way of even introducing myself. None of it was comfortable. All of it, looking back, was necessary.

Eighteen-plus years in, I could tell you it gets easier. It doesn't, not exactly — it just changes shape. Right now, the "new" is artificial intelligence and large language models, and once again I find myself a beginner, relearning how to build things, this time alongside tools that didn't exist for most of my career. It's humbling in the same way Dubai was humbling, and Sydney was humbling. And somehow that keeps being the most interesting part.

If there's one thing I'd want anyone reading this to take away — technical background or not — it's this: you don't need the whole plan before you start. I didn't have one. I had a direction, a willingness to be uncomfortable, and enough stubbornness to keep going after the parts that didn't work out. The wild dream doesn't arrive all at once. It arrives in the decision to take the next uncomfortable step, over and over, for years, without much proof along the way that it's going to work out.

It did work out — so far. But I want to be honest about the "so far," because I think that's the actual lesson. I'm not writing this from some finish line, looking back at a completed journey. I'm writing it from the middle of it. The dream that once felt impossibly far away became my everyday life, and instead of stopping there, it just... kept going. New chapters keep showing up. I don't think that ever really stops, and I've stopped expecting it to.

So if you're standing where I once stood — unsure, maybe scared, holding something that feels more like a wild dream than a plan — that feeling doesn't mean you're on the wrong path. It might just mean you're at the beginning of one.

I'm still on mine.
